SUPERVISOR NOTES
====================================================

These are the areas I expect to be weakest when you
first attempt this checklist, based on our session:

1. QUATERNION MATHS (Part E2):
   Most students can use quatDiff() but cannot explain
   WHY the formula is qd = q2 * conjugate(q1) or where
   the 2*arccos comes from. This is fine for now —
   the important thing is knowing what the output means
   (radians of angular difference). Deep quaternion math
   can come later.

2. REWARD DESIGN INTUITION (Part F3):
   Understanding each reward TERM is easier than
   understanding how they INTERACT. The key insight is
   that the drift penalty and the gate are both there
   to prevent the agent from "multi-tasking badly."
   If you can explain THAT, you understand the reward.

3. OFF-POLICY / REPLAY BUFFER (Part C3):
   This is a subtle concept. The key is: SAC does NOT
   only learn from what just happened. It can learn from
   experiences collected 50,000 steps ago. This is why
   the replay buffer matters and why SAC is efficient.

4. CURRICULUM INTUITION (Part G):
   Students often understand curriculum learning
   intellectually but not WHY it helps mathematically.
   The reason: sparse rewards early in training mean
   the gradient signal is zero for most episodes, so
   the agent doesn't improve. Curriculum = more non-zero
   reward signal = faster learning.

5. WHAT TO DO AFTER PHASE 2 (Part I3):
   This is where most students freeze. The answer is:
   evaluate first, then decide. Never change the code
   before you know what the current results actually are.
